Design teardown & rebuild

The Collective,
taken apart.

Everything recoverable from a 114-second screen recording of thecollective.in, cross-checked against the site's own compiled stylesheet — then rebuilt as a working storefront for a fictional Delhi couture house.

Source 1918×864, 30fps, 114s Frames read 114 CSS audited ~600 KB Deliverable 8-page storefront

Method

Two sources, cross-checked

The recording gives layout, proportion and interaction. The compiled CSS gives exact values. Neither alone is enough — the site's HTML ships as an empty skeleton.

Both product and category pages bail out of server rendering (BAILOUT_TO_CLIENT_SIDE_RENDERING), so the downloaded HTML contains nothing but grey MUI skeleton blocks. What survived intact was the stylesheet: fourteen files, roughly 600 KB, carrying every CSS-module class for the PDP, PLP, header, footer and checkout. Numbers below marked measured come from that stylesheet; numbers marked derived are read off frames and scaled from the 1440-wide capture to the 1918-wide viewport (×1.332).

One structural finding worth stating plainly: the site has no design tokens. Three CSS custom properties exist in the whole codebase, all belonging to the Swiper library. Every colour, size and spacing value is hard-coded at the point of use — which is why the same grey appears as #ccc, #cccccc and silver in different modules.

Type

Four weights of one family, plus a wordmark serif

The rebuild

What was actually shipped

The system above, applied to a fictional Delhi couture house — Vrinda Sethi — with a full purchase path and a designer's-story section the original has no equivalent for.

8
Pages: home, women, men, product, bag, order, atelier, plus the listing engine shared by both categories
29
Products across 8 sub-categories, each with real copy, fabric, craft and per-size stock
34/34
Self-test assertions passing over catalogue integrity, cart maths and render helpers
48/48
Responsive checks clean — every page at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1180 and 1440

Fidelity to the source

Same Lato cuts, self-hosted from the original build. Same colour ladder, same radius-0 shape language, same .3s ease, same inverted button pair, same mixed-weight section headings, same sticky-filter PLP and sticky-panel PDP, same three-step checkout with the green stepper, same white-slab toasts.

Deliberate departures

Tokens instead of literals
The whole palette and scale sit in :root custom properties. Retheming is one block, not a search-and-replace.
One product card
Rather than the original's three-and-a-half implementations.
Two breakpoints
1180 and 1024, consistently, instead of nine.
A display serif
Cormorant Garamond for the wordmark and editorial headlines — a single-designer house needs a voice the multi-brand retailer deliberately avoids.
The Atelier
An entire page with no counterpart in the source: the designer's story, five named craft clusters, a five-stage made-to-measure process, press and appointments.
Mobile PDP gallery
A swipeable snap carousel rather than a stack of six full-width images.
Everything is fictional and local. The house, its products, prices and stock are invented. Photography and footage are Pexels-licensed and stored in the repo — no runtime network calls. Checkout takes no payment and places no real order; bag, wishlist and address live only in localStorage.
